Board questions to ask

  • A familiar pawn break is becoming possible for either side.
  • One side has delayed development to defend or attack a central pawn.
  • A piece is heading to a standard square because it supports the structure, not because the square is always good.

Core practical rules

  1. Identify the central pawn structure before copying a familiar setup.
  2. Finish development with purpose: every piece should support a break, defend the king, or pressure a target.
  3. Know the opponent's freeing move or counterbreak and treat it as part of your own opening knowledge.
  4. Do not spend tempi winning a pawn if the opponent gains development, open lines, or a safer king.
  5. When the structure changes, stop playing opening moves from memory and re-evaluate the position.
  6. For Modern Benoni, always restate the position in one sentence before moving: what is the main asset, the main danger, and the next useful transformation?

Common ways players misuse the idea

  • Memorizing a sequence without knowing what to do after the first deviation.
  • Moving the same piece repeatedly while the rest of the army remains undeveloped.
  • Ignoring the opponent's central break because your own setup is incomplete.
  • Winning a pawn while losing king safety or development.
  • Continuing an opening plan after pawn exchanges have created a different structure.
  • Treating Modern Benoni as a memorized label instead of a position that must be recalculated when pieces or pawns change.
