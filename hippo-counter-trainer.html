The anti-Hippo decision map

What White showsYour default reactionWhat you are trying to prevent
d4-e4-f4 before you finish the shellAbort the autopilot. Develop ...Nf6 and play a Pirc/Modern-style fight for the center.White using the extra tempo to blast open a cramped undeveloped position.
c4-d4-e4 broad centerTransform early. ...Nf6 is the cleanest sign that you are no longer insisting on a pure Hippo.Being squeezed while White owns too much central space.
White commits d5Usually meet it with ...e5, then prepare the ...f5 lever.Leaving the center fluid while your pieces have little room.
White commits e5Usually meet it with ...d5, then look for ...c5.Letting White gain space without giving yourself a counter-break.
g4-g5 pawn stormWhen tactically safe, ...h5 can lock that wing. Then counter in the center.Opening files directly against your king for free.
h4-h5 hits g6When tactically safe, ...g5 closes the contact. Do not treat castling as mandatory.Allowing hxg6 to rip open the kingside on White's terms.
Be3-Qd2-h4 / obvious opposite-wing attackDo not castle by reflex. Hit the center with ...c5 (or ...d5 when appropriate) and decide king placement later.Walking your king into the attack White has already announced.
Nothing has actually committed yetKeep flexibility. A waiting move is often better than forcing a break just because the setup is complete.Helping White by declaring your structure first.
Important: these are memory anchors, not forced engine moves. The Hippo is a flexible family of positions, so tactics and move order can override the rule. The goal is to stop the most common failure mode: completing the pretty formation after White has already played a setup that makes that formation strategically inappropriate.
